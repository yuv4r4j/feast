01 · RAW DATA

It starts with rows

Three tables in Snowflake. Each dot is one row: 48 loans, 16 borrowers, 12 pieces of collateral.

The warehouse knows their column types. It has no idea what any of it means, which is why every dot is grey.

02 · THE PROBLEM

Same question, three answers

A dashboard, a notebook and a chatbot each write their own SQL for “total exposure”. One includes undrawn limits, one doesn't, one double-counts joint borrowers.

The data is fine. The meaning lives in people's heads, and everything that follows is about writing it down.

All figures in this story are made up for illustration.

03 · TAXONOMY

Start by sorting

A taxonomy is the simplest kind of meaning: a tree of categories. Corporate is a kind of Borrower, which is a kind of Party. The 16 borrower rows drop into their leaf.

It has one kind of link, broader to narrower, so it is good for roll-ups and tagging and silent on everything else. In Snowflake it is usually a parent-child table, level columns, or tags.

04 · ONTOLOGY

Then say how things relate

An ontology keeps the taxonomy's tree and adds what a tree can't say: the other relationships between things, and the rules. A Borrower is a Party. A Guarantor guarantees a Loan. A Loan is secured by Collateral.

Notice the rows fade out: an ontology holds no data. Snowflake has no ontology object either. Teams keep it in OWL/RDF or YAML, in catalog descriptions, or in partner tools.

05 · SEMANTIC VIEW

Then tell Snowflake

CREATE SEMANTIC VIEW wraps the physical tables as logical tables, declares which keys join them, and attaches synonyms and descriptions taken from the ontology.

It is a real schema-level object, granted and governed like any view. It stores definitions, not rows. The dots take on colour because Snowflake now knows what each table is.

06 · DIMENSIONS AND FACTS

Dimensions sort, facts measure

A dimension is something you slice by. Group by borrowers.segment and the borrowers line up into Retail, SME and Corporate.

Their loans follow them. Nobody wrote a join: the relationship declared in the last step did it.

A fact is a row-level number, here loans.ead, shown as dot size.

07 · METRICS

The formula, written once

A metric is an aggregation over facts: total_ead = SUM(loans.ead). The loans stack into one column per segment.

This is the line that ends the argument from step two. The formula lives in the view, so nobody re-derives it in their own SQL.

08 · SEMANTIC LAYER

That is a semantic layer

Step back. The rows drop to the bottom, consumers sit on top, and one governed set of definitions sits between them. That middle tier is the semantic layer.

It is a pattern, not a product. The semantic view is Snowflake's native way to build it; dbt Semantic Layer, LookML and Cube are others. Either way, every consumer now gets the same number.

09 · USING THE LAYER

Ask in plain language

Cortex Analyst reads the view's names, synonyms and metrics, so it knows “exposure” means total_ead. It writes a query against the semantic view rather than guessing at raw tables.

Snowflake expands that into the joins and the SUM, runs it on the physical tables, and the answer comes back.

10 · KNOWLEDGE GRAPH

A question a sum can't answer

“If Acme Corp fails, who else is exposed?” That is a question about paths, not totals.

So the same rows rearrange into a knowledge graph: every borrower, loan and collateral item becomes a node, every relationship an edge, each typed by the ontology.

In Snowflake that means node and edge tables with recursive SQL, or a Native App such as RelationalAI or Neo4j Graph Analytics.

11 · TRAVERSAL

Follow the links

Start at Acme. One hop out: the firm that guarantees Acme's loan, a firm whose loan Acme guarantees, and a firm that pledged the same warehouse.

Two hops out: their guarantees and shared collateral. Seven counterparties are tied to Acme, and none of that shows up in a bar chart.

12 · TOGETHER

Five ideas, one stack

Taxonomy is the tree of categories, and the backbone of the ontology.

Ontology is the dictionary. It informs the view and gives the graph its schema.

Semantic view is that meaning stored as a Snowflake object, with metrics on top.

Semantic layer is the pattern the view implements: one definition for every consumer.

Knowledge graph is the same rows as connected instances, for questions about who links to whom.

Aggregates go through the view. Paths go through the graph. An agent can use both.
